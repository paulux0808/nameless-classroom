# Stage 2 playability review

Keep landscape movement and the joystick. Chapters share controls, not the same puzzle or room treatment.

- Compose the opening camera around the first meaningful object. Check its projection and its real appearance. A task sentence is not a substitute for staging.
- Use light, facing, reachable placement and physical changes to show what can be investigated. Signs must belong to an actual object; never attach a billboard to every hotspot.
- Repeated character models do not represent distinct people. CH1 and CH2 use separate Quaternius meshes and rigs. Add future principal actors with distinct source models, not recolors.
- Stage dialogue as exchanges with the player: each beat may direct gaze, head motion and a short pause. An error-specific response should acknowledge the actual record instead of repeating a grading phrase.
- CH1 is a warm, timber-floored calculation office; CH2 is a high, steel-framed communal test hall. Their desks, seating, storage, lights and architecture differ, beyond rearranging furniture.
- Do not fill the initial object view with unavailable actions. The first interaction must produce readable content and a visible response.
- Object panels preserve the room view. CH2 uses a right panel of at most 430px / 53% of a landscape viewport, without full-screen blur. Reduce margins before shrinking text or touch targets.
- Test 568×320 and 844×390 with the real modal ancestors and CSS, including stamps and receipts. An isolated component can hide a backdrop or container bug.
- Walk the opening and the complete dependency chain. Check early exploration, incorrect attempts, recovery, changing an accepted setting, and final exit access.
- Logic and DOM checks cannot certify a 3D scene. The CPU preview uses the actual scene and camera for composition only; GPU textures, lighting and shadows still need a WebGL-capable device.

CH2's opening is a lamp-lit spread of four envelopes beside Enrico. The papers introduce the disagreement. Their facility memo points to the cabinet; the machine's loose plate provides the date. Cabinet parts fit the machine's visibly empty sockets. Opening drawers, moving papers, emitted records and a changing work light replace the persistent task sentence.
